How it works

Polymarket runs temperature markets on 50 cities. Eleven brackets, one wins. We forecast the whole distribution and compare it to what the market is actually charging. Where the gap is worth taking, we post.

Market discovery

Every live market, automatically

No fixed city list. The engine reads the market feed and extracts the brackets, the unit, the deadline and the resolution rules — including which station the market actually settles on. New cities appear the day they list.

Probability engine

A distribution, not a guess

A large multi-model ensemble, read at the exact station in the rules — not the city centre, which can be a full bracket away. Every bracket gets a probability. They sum to 100%.

Executable prices

The ask, not the sticker

The displayed percentage is not a price you can fill. Every leg is checked against the live order book. If it can't absorb the order, there is no signal.

Portfolio construction

One quote, whichever lands

3–5 neighbouring brackets, split so the payout is identical whichever one is the day's high. One number to read, one outcome to check.

Most markets produce nothing. The engine stays silent when the edge is thin, the book is shallow, the spread is wide, or the models disagree. A channel posting twenty signals a day is posting fifteen bad ones.

The only claim worth making

Not return. Not win rate. Both are easy to fake and easy to get by accident. The question is simpler:

When we say 90%, does it happen 90% of the time?

Robustness

The edge doesn't move

We swept the model's confidence setting across its entire plausible range. Hit rate swung from 77% to 96%. The edge over the market barely moved. An overfit wanders with its parameters. This doesn't.

Control

Buying blind loses 38%

Same markets, same period, no engine — just buy the cheapest block every day: −38.1%. The extremes are expensive and the middle is cheap. That gap is what the engine is built to find.

Correction

We measured our own overconfidence

Across all markets we're calibrated to a fraction of a point. On the ones we select, we were meaningfully more confident than we had earned — picking the best of many options selects for your own optimism. We measured it and now subtract it before publishing. Fewer signals. Honest ones.
